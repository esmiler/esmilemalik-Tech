How it works

Four steps, and one of them is yours.

You send the brief

The words themselves, or the story told plainly. Who or what it is about, the feeling you want it to carry, the occasion, and when you need it. Nothing needs to be tidy.

We agree the shape

Format (a spoken-word piece over a soundscape bed, or a fully composed song with vocals), length, tone, and licence. Scope, price and delivery date are confirmed in writing before work starts.

It gets made

Refined, arranged, produced and finished with the same care the catalogue gets. You hear a draft and get one round of direction before the final.

You receive it

Mastered audio, artwork if you want it, and a written record of how it was made and exactly what rights you hold.

Formats

Two ways your words can be set.

Priced by complexity — the writing, arrangement and personalisation involved, not the running time.

Spoken word over a soundscape

Your words, read aloud — by you or by a voice we agree on — set over a bed built to sit underneath them. The most direct route from a story to something you can play to someone.

A fully composed song

Lyrics developed into a structured piece with vocals, arrangement and production. More writing, more iteration, more of your input — and meaningfully more involved to make.

Pricing

Tiers are priced individually against the brief, because the difference between a three-minute spoken piece and a fully written song is genuine. You get a fixed quote in writing before anything begins — never an open meter.

Before you send

Two things worth knowing.

The words have to be yours to give. Your own life story is yours. A poem someone else wrote is not, and neither are song lyrics you love but did not write. The brief form asks you to confirm this, and it is the one thing that cannot be worked around — it protects you as much as me.

Production is AI-assisted, and that is disclosed. Generation is a tool in the studio; the direction, refinement, arrangement and finishing are human. Your contribution — the words — is recorded in the rights ledger as your material, and your licence terms are written down and handed over with the file.

The brief

Tell me the story.

Take as much room as you need. The more of the feeling you can put into words here, the closer the first draft will land.

Please don’t include anything you would not want read by another person, or anyone else’s private information.
